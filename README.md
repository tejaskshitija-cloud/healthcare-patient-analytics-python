
# Healthcare Patient Analytics Using Python & Pandas

## Project Overview

This project analyzes 1,000 synthetic healthcare patient records using Python and Pandas to identify patterns in patient demographics, diagnoses, pain scores, treatment utilization and treatment outcomes.

The project demonstrates practical application of healthcare data analytics and converts patient-level data into meaningful clinical and management insights.

## Objectives

* Analyze patient demographics and diagnosis distribution
* Examine pain patterns across diagnoses and gender
* Analyze treatment utilization
* Evaluate observed treatment improvement rates
* Identify monthly recovery patterns
* Create healthcare data visualizations
* Generate actionable insights from patient data

## Dataset

The dataset contains **1,000 synthetic patient records** and includes:

* Patient ID
* Age
* Gender
* Diagnosis
* Pain Score
* Visit Date
* Treatment
* Outcome
* Comorbidity
* Length of Treatment
* Department

The dataset is synthetic and does not contain real patient information.

## Key Analysis

### Patient Profile

The dataset contains 1,000 patients with ages ranging from 18 to 85 years. The average patient age is approximately 51 years.

### Diagnosis Analysis

Stroke had the highest patient volume with 164 patients, while Low Back Pain had the lowest with 120 patients.

### Pain Analysis

Stroke showed the highest observed average pain score at approximately 5.80, while Knee Osteoarthritis showed the lowest at approximately 5.46.

### Treatment Outcomes

ROM Exercises showed the highest observed overall improvement rate at **66.7%**, followed by Manual Therapy at **62.6%** and Gait Training at **61.2%**.

### Monthly Recovery Analysis

In May 2025, Balance Training recorded **9 improved patients out of 12**, representing a **75% observed improvement rate**.

In July 2026, Manual Therapy recorded **8 improved patients out of 8**. Because the sample size was smaller, this finding should be interpreted cautiously.

## Key Insight

The analysis demonstrates how healthcare data can be transformed into structured insights about patient populations, clinical conditions, treatment utilization and observed outcomes.

These findings describe patterns in the synthetic dataset and should not be interpreted as evidence of treatment effectiveness or causal relationships.
